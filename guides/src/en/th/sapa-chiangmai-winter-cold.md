# How cold are Sapa and Chiang Mai in winter? What to pack for Dec–Jan

This guide is from Yetu SEA Travel Weather. Many travellers expect short-sleeve weather all year in Southeast Asia. Sapa in Vietnam and Chiang Mai in Thailand both sit in the north and do get cold in December and January — but differently: **Sapa is damp mountain cold; Chiang Mai is hot by day and cool at night.**

## Sapa: damp mountain cold, possibly near 0°C

According to QCVN 02:2022/BXD, the Sa Pa station averages 13.2°C by day and 7.0°C at night in December, and 12.3°C and 6.2°C in January, with about 14 and 16 rain days — cold and damp. The same regulation lists extreme minimums of −3.2°C in December and −4.2°C in January (table A.6). Vietnam’s national tourism authority says the same:

- Sapa is cool all year with an average temperature of about 15°C; in winter it is often foggy, temperatures may drop to 0°C, and snow sometimes falls ([Vietnam National Authority of Tourism](https://vietnamtourism.vn/en/index.php/about/items/2534)).
- December to March is chilly, misty and cloudy, especially from late December; January sees occasional snowfall, and hotels provide heated blankets ([tourism authority climate page](https://vietnam.travel/things-to-do/weather-and-climate-vietnam/)).

For comparison, Hanoi averages a 14.6°C low in January, and the tourism authority says Hanoi itself can drop to around 10°C from late December to March. Sapa is much colder than Hanoi — pack for real winter.

**What to wear in Sapa:**

- A thermal base layer, a fleece or sweater, and a windproof, waterproof shell — or a down jacket.
- Long trousers, ideally one warmer or layerable pair.
- Waterproof, grippy hiking shoes for wet, misty trails.
- Hat, gloves and thick socks; ask your hotel about heated blankets, which the tourism authority says local hotels provide.

Yetu does not cover Sapa. Before you go, check forecasts and cold-spell notices from Vietnam’s national forecasting centre ([nchmf.gov.vn](https://nchmf.gov.vn/)); if you pass through Hanoi, you can check Hanoi’s 7-day forecast on Yetu.

## Chiang Mai: hot days, cool nights

Chiang Mai averages 29.2°C by day and 16.9°C at night in December, and 30.0°C and 15.7°C in January — a daily swing of roughly 12–14°C. In 1991–2020 the lowest readings were 3.8°C in December and 8.7°C in January (extreme minimums in the same TMD table), so cold-air outbreaks can make early mornings genuinely cold.

These figures come from the city station at 305 m. Mountains such as Doi Suthep and Doi Inthanon are colder, so bring an extra layer for trips up.

**What to wear in Chiang Mai:**

- Daytime: short sleeves, light trousers or a skirt, and sun protection.
- Mornings, night markets, sunrise trips: long sleeves or a light jacket; add a fleece or light down for the mountains.
- Temples ask for covered shoulders and knees, so long trousers or a long skirt do double duty.
- December and January average only 1–2 rain days, so rain gear is not a priority.

A few days before you go, you can check Chiang Mai’s 7-day forecast and weather-based outfit suggestions on Yetu.

## Packing for both

1. Base for Chiang Mai days: two or three short-sleeve shirts and light trousers.
2. Mid layer: two long-sleeve shirts plus one fleece or sweater — useful in both places.
3. Outer layer: a windproof, waterproof shell or down jacket for Sapa; a light jacket is enough for Chiang Mai alone.
4. Shoes: hiking shoes for Sapa, sandals or light shoes for Chiang Mai.
5. Extras: hat and gloves for Sapa, sun kit for Chiang Mai.
